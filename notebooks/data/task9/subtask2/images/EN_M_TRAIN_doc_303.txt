Ever fantasized about having an entire hotel to yourself while on vacation? The Four Seasons Resort Bora Bora can make that dream come true: For $2.75 million, guests can buy out the entire island property for a week, dining at its open-air restaurants, snorkeling in the lagoon, and playing tennis with the hotel’s pro. Or, you could invite up to 245 of your closest friends to stay with you in the resort’s seven villas and 108 bungalow suites.

The annual Henley Passport Index ranks all 199 global passports based on the number of countries a holder can visit without needing a visa. Once again, Singapore is the world’s most powerful passport for 2024—providing visa-free access to 194 countries—but this year, four European nations (France, Germany, Italy and Spain) are tied for No. 1. The United States is tied for seventh place this year with access to 188 countries.

In the Eternal City, even hotels have interesting past lives. Rome’s newly opened Palazzo Ripetta—a short walk from the Spanish Steps and Via del Corso—was built in the 17th century as a convent. For three centuries, the Conservatory of Divine Providence housed orphaned girls and young women, but last year the property was converted into a luxury boutique hotel. Today, Palazzo Ripetta has 78 rooms and suites, a rooftop bar, and the San Baylon restaurant, overseen by Marco Ciccotelli, one of the most talented young chefs in Rome.
