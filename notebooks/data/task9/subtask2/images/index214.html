How To Use AI-Driven Compliance And Fraud Detection In Real Estate

Marc Rutzen serves as cofounder and CEO of HelloData.ai.

With the proliferation of artificial intelligence, everyone from Multiple Listing Service (MLS) providers to the end user—renter or buyer—will begin to face issues not previously encountered in the world of real estate. Deceptively photoshopped images, AI-generated descriptions and misrepresentations in listings are among the problems beginning to surface as the adoption of AI tools increases.

But even as tech produces problems, it also creates solutions. Understanding how to also use AI tools to ensure compliance and prevent fraud can help MLS providers avoid serious liability issues. In this article, I’ll walk you through how AI can help with accurate data extraction and comparison, watermark and signage detection, altered image detection, AI-generated description detection and fair housing compliance.

Data Extraction And Comparison

There are tools available, such as data extraction and analysis tools, to help automate the due diligence process in the real estate industry. Using computer vision technology and AI, robust structured data can be extracted from real estate images, documents and websites. Using comparison processes, the AI tool will automatically flag inconsistencies.

For example, if a listing indicates it was recently renovated, but the pictures show spaces that are of poor quality/condition, it will be flagged. Or if a listing mentions a pool in its amenity list, but there isn't a single photo of a pool, the tool would point that out.

Watermark & Signage Detection

The inappropriate use of images with logos or watermarks is on the rise. Whether it’s a brand mix-up or a listing with malicious intent, such as a fraudulent listing looking to scam buyers, both companies and consumers are becoming victims.

These problems can be avoided by using AI to assist in the detection and flagging of inconsistencies and duplications. The technology can spot things like competitors' logos or signage on a listing. For example, if Coldwell Banker's logo or signage is shown on a ReMax listing, algorithms would detect and flag it, helping MLS providers avoid fines for logos or advertising misuse.

Altered Image Detection

Deception is not always easily identifiable, as the capabilities of photo-editing tools are continually increasing.. Images can be altered to make a listing more appealing to buyers, and these favorable edits are not always apparent to the human eye.

AI image analysis and computer vision have the ability to detect altered images in property listings and will then flag them for review. The use of this tool will ensure the authenticity of images and prevent the sharing of misleading information with potential buyers or tenants.

AI-Generated Description Detection

ChatGPT has recently rocketed into the spotlight, and its uses are nearly unlimited. Despite its numerous benefits, the caveat of text generation is inaccuracy—especially in real estate listings. Fraudulent listings that used to clearly sound like fraud can now pass for legitimate if run through ChatGPT.

But just as AI can be used to generate property descriptions, it can be used to detect those AI generations. Automated detection can uncover unwanted, inaccurate or even counterfeit content to prevent costly and embarrassing mistakes in listings.

Fair Housing Compliance

AI is a great tool for monitoring fair housing compliance. It does this by using Large Language Models to learn and detect not only keywords but even the context of the text. This sophisticated compliance monitoring system ensures listings adhere to the rules of the Fair Housing Act.

The benefits of AI in compliance may also extend to document cross-checking and verification—a crucial aspect of the loan submission process. Machine learning algorithms could cross-examine various documents submitted during a loan application process and look for critical values such as operating statements, rent rolls, signed documents, appraisal valuation dates and other data that could be compared across documents to ensure consistency and reduce the risk of fraud. This process could significantly streamline loan submissions to organizations like Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, leading to increased efficiency and reduced risk.

While the real estate world adapts to the increased use of AI, these innovative applications of the technology will ensure accuracy, reduce the occurrence of fraud and even simplify compliance. AI will remain both the problem and answer as it reshapes real estate into a more efficient industry.
