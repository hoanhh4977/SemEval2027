TurboTax Deceived Customers By Promising ‘Free’ Tax Prep, FTC Says

Topline

The Federal Trade Commission has found Intuit, the maker of TurboTax, used deceptive advertising techniques by promoting “free” tax preparation services that weren’t actually free for all customers, the FTC said Monday, issuing an order to rein in some of the company’s marketing practices.

Key Facts

The FTC found Intuit’s claims that TurboTax offered a free service weren’t substantiated, noting most tax filers weren’t eligible for the free version of the tax filing website and needed to upgrade to premium products.

The commission also found the deceptive marketing tactics have lasted for years and continue today as Intuit has continued to run ads that “led consumers to believe that they could file their returns for free.”

The order filed against Inuit requires it to stop advertising its services as free unless they are free for every consumer or the company explains what percentage of consumers qualify for the free service.

Intuit will also have to disclose the “terms, conditions and obligations” required for consumers to use their “free” goods or services.

The FTC voted 3-0 to issue the opinion and order against Intuit.

Contra

“Absolutely no one should be surprised that FTC Commissioners — employees of the FTC — ruled in favor of the FTC as they have done in every appeal for the last two decades,” an Intuit spokesperson told Forbes. “This decision is the result of a biased and broken system where the Commission serves as accuser, judge, jury, and then appellate judge all in the same case.” Intuit said it will appeal the decision to a federal circuit court.

Key Background

The FTC first sued Intuit for deceptive advertising in early 2022, accusing the company of tricking consumers with bogus advertisements claiming TurboTax offered “free” tax filing services that millions couldn’t use because they worked as independent contractors or took certain tax deductions. An internal FTC judge ruled against Intuit last year, and the company appealed the decision to the FTC’s three commissioners, arguing that the specific ads challenged by the FTC said the free offers were only available to consumers with “simple tax returns.” The company also argued at the time the ads were found deceptive by the administrative law judge by “disregarding or improperly construing evidence,” adding the order “unconstitutionally compels speech.”

Big Number

About two-thirds. That’s the share of tax-filers who couldn’t use TurboTax’s free product, according to an FTC case summary.

Tangent

Intuit isn’t paying any sort of penalty or settlement as part of the case, though other deceptive advertising cases have resulted in multi-million dollar costs for large companies. Uber was ordered to pay $20 million in 2017 to settle claims it misled drivers about how much they could make through being a driver for the ride-hailing service. Grand Canyon University was fined a record $37.7 million last month for falsely advertising the cost of some of its doctoral programs. The falsely advertised costs were up to 98% less than what students ended up paying.
