Zelensky Cancels U.S. Senate Address Amid Ukraine Aid Dispute

Topline

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has canceled his address to the U.S. Senate, Senate Majority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-N.Y.) said Tuesday, as funding for Ukraine aid has been held up by House Republicans demanding additional border funding.

Key Facts

Schumer said Zelensky canceled his virtual appearance at the Senate’s closed-door briefing on Ukraine and Israel on Tuesday after “something came up at the last minute,” according to Politico.

Contra

Senate Minority Leader Mitch McConnell (R-Ky.) said Tuesday he is urging House Republicans to vote against the Ukraine supplemental aid package unless “meaningful” border security changes are included.

Key Background

President Joe Biden is requesting $61.4 billion in aid for Ukraine as part of a $105 billion package that includes funding for Israel, Taiwan, U.S. allies in the Indo-Pacific and border agents and facilities at the U.S.-Mexico border. Schumer signaled Sunday that he planned to bring the Biden administration’s supplemental aid package for a vote this week. The White House sent a letter to congressional leaders Monday indicating the U.S. was “out of money” and “nearly out of time” to support Ukraine’s efforts against Russia. Republicans have demanded that anti-immigration policies be tied to Ukraine aid, including new asylum restrictions, continued construction of a border wall and more Border Patrol agents. House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-La.) said last week he’s “confident and optimistic” that Congress will approve aid for Israel and Ukraine before the end of the year.
